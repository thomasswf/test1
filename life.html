Life & Travel

Home is not just
one city.

I was born in Guangdong, grew up further in Hong Kong, and now call Tokyo home. Those places shaped how I see cities, food, language and the idea of belonging. Travel, for me, is less about collecting destinations than noticing how people live.

Places that shaped me

A life across South China and Japan.

Guangdong

Where the story began

Cantonese culture, food and family remain part of the baseline through which I understand other places. Even after years abroad, returning south still feels different from travelling somewhere new.

Hong Kong

A city that taught me speed and adaptability

I spent formative school and working years in Hong Kong. It remains a place of family, old friends, dense streets, late meals and a rhythm that instantly feels familiar whenever I return.

Tokyo

The city I chose to build a life in

Tokyo rewards attention: the order of a station, a neighborhood bathhouse, the design of a tiny restaurant, the way seasons change what people eat and where they go. Living here has made me appreciate quiet systems as much as spectacular landmarks.

Travel notes

Japan is best understood slowly.

Mountains & snow country

Winter travel is often tied to snowboarding, but the journey itself matters too: local trains, mountain towns, hot food after a cold day, and the change in pace once Tokyo disappears behind you.

Food as a map

Regional food makes geography memorable. Sanuki udon in Kagawa, neighborhood izakaya, seafood near the coast — meals are often the easiest way into a place.

Festivals & local rhythm

Fireworks, seasonal events and local gatherings show a side of Japan that is difficult to understand from major-city tourism alone.

Travel by bicycle

Cycling changes scale. Distances become physical, coastlines become continuous, and towns that would be invisible from a train become part of the journey.

Languages & perspective

Different languages reveal different versions of a place.

Cantonese, Mandarin, English and Japanese each belong to a different part of my life. Switching language often changes not only how I communicate, but what I notice — especially when moving between Hong Kong, mainland China and Japan.
