Hobbies · GALA Yuzawa · March 15, 2025

Movement keeps me curious.

Snowboarding, hiking, cycling and the quieter things I practise for no reason other than wanting to. They reset my attention, keep me learning without a KPI, and make life feel larger than work.

Outside work

Movement, craft and curiosity.

01 · Snow

Snowboarding

Snowboarding is one of the clearest ways I switch off from work. Mountains demand attention to weather, terrain and movement — and reward it immediately. I enjoy both the technical progression and the simple feeling of a full day outside in winter.

02 · Endurance

Autumn and winter on the same trail. This is the only hike where I have seen autumn foliage and settled snow at the same time. The overlap of two seasons made Mount Tanigawa one of my most memorable hikes.

Slow pace, fast ride. In one afternoon, I completed the approximately 60 km loop around Lake Hamana on my own. This photo was taken at the rental-bike starting point, just before setting off.

03 · Seeing

Photography & architecture

I’m drawn to structure, repetition, engineering detail and the way light changes a city. I prefer photographs that preserve a sense of place over images created only to look impressive.

04 · Music

Piano

Piano is almost the opposite of engineering work: improvement comes from repetition, listening and touch. It is slow progress, which is exactly why I value it.

05 · Systems at home

Energy, automation & smart home

I enjoy treating the home as a small engineering system — solar generation, battery storage, energy monitoring, automation and home servers. The goal is not gadgets for their own sake, but making energy use visible and letting useful routines disappear into the background.

06 · Mobility

EVs & technology

Electric vehicles interest me as part of a larger shift in energy, software and infrastructure. I enjoy the engineering trade-offs as much as the driving experience: charging, efficiency, batteries and how transport integrates with the home.

What connects them

I like systems you can learn by doing.

Mountains, music, machines and cities all become more interesting when you understand a little more about how they work.
